# Mixed-Methods Workflow for Management Research

## 1. Choose the Design

| Design | Use When | Integration Point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exploratory sequential | The construct, mechanism, or phenomenon is under-theorized. | Qualitative findings define constructs, hypotheses, measures, and boundary conditions. |
| Explanatory sequential | Quantitative results are strong but the mechanism is unclear. | Follow-up interviews/cases explain why the pattern appears. |
| Convergent parallel | Two evidence systems can be collected independently. | Joint display compares convergence, complementarity, and dissonance. |
| Embedded | One method is primary and the other reduces a specific inferential risk. | Auxiliary evidence strengthens measurement, mechanism, or external validity. |

## 2. Turn Qualitative Findings into Quantitative Hypotheses

1. Name the first-order observation in participant language.
2. Abstract it into a second-order theme.
3. Link the theme to a management theory.
4. Convert the theme into a measurable construct.
5. Specify the expected relationship.
6. State the boundary condition.
7. Identify an alternative explanation and a falsification test.

## 3. Turn Quantitative Results Back into Mechanism

1. Separate robust empirical pattern from interpretation.
2. Ask which qualitative evidence explains the pattern.
3. Use quotes to illuminate mechanism, not to restate coefficients.
4. Explain null or mixed results as boundary conditions when justified.
5. Avoid claiming causal mechanisms if the design only supports association.

## 4. Joint Display

A useful joint display has at least five columns:

- Qualitative insight.
- Representative evidence.
- Quantitative operationalization.
- Empirical result.
- Integrated inference.

The last column is the point. Without integrated inference, the table is only a side-by-side appendix.

## 5. Idea to Story to Paper

### Idea

- What surprising phenomenon would a top management scholar care about?
- What existing theory predicts poorly or incompletely?
- What mechanism can be named precisely?

### Story

- Tension: what is puzzling?
- Lens: which theory helps but is incomplete?
- Mechanism: what process explains the puzzle?
- Evidence: why does mixed evidence fit the mechanism?
- Contribution: what changes in how the field thinks?

### Paper

- Introduction opens with the puzzle, not the method.
- Theory section uses qualitative evidence to sharpen constructs, not to replace theory.
- Methods section explains sequencing, sampling, measurement, and integration.
- Results section alternates evidence only when integration improves inference.
- Discussion states what the mixed design reveals that either method alone could not.

## 6. Reviewer Risks

- The qualitative part looks decorative.
- The quantitative part ignores constructs produced by qualitative analysis.
- The two samples do not speak to the same population or phenomenon.
- The paper reports two separate studies but never integrates them.
- The story uses mixed methods to compensate for weak identification.
- The contribution is framed as “we used mixed methods” instead of a theoretical insight.
