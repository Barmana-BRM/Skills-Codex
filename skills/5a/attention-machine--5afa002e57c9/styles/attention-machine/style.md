# Attention Machine

Feels like a confident friend holding up proof as they talk, in the brand's colours. Calm cutting, real proof on screen, heavy type and a clear brand palette.

General is the whole look. The shape sections after it only say what differs in that shape.

## General

### Colours


| Name      | Hex       | Job                                                                                            |
| --------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary   | `#0731F8` | The main colour: the block behind headlines over footage, list numbers, the term line's border |
| Secondary | `#FED347` | The payoff: the last big word of a word stack, or the number the story builds to               |
| Cream     | `#F5F1EA` | Background behind dark icons, text and motion graphics                                         |
| Dark      | `#050505` | Background behind light icons, text and motion graphics, and section cards                     |
| White     | `#FFFFFF` | Captions and small text over footage, with a faint soft shadow; headline text on blue blocks   |


- Overlays and clips keep their own colours.
- **Blue blocks:** headlines over footage sit on a solid primary block hugging the text: padding about 10% of the text size top and bottom and 35% each side, corners about 18%. One block per line, about 10% of the text size apart. No shadow on the text. Text on a dark or cream card or panel has no block: white on dark, dark on cream.
- The dark background has a barely visible lift in the middle and a still grain.

### Type

- **Headlines:** Unbounded Bold. Word stacks, big numbers, titles, section cards, points in a list.
- **Text:** Poppins Bold for captions and small words. Poppins Medium for plain-words lines.
- **Code:** JetBrains Mono Medium.

Unbounded and Poppins are free on Google Fonts. Unbounded is very wide: size it to fit the space it has, not a fixed number. Sizes are in the shape sections.

### Text on screen

- **Word stack:** the core idea in 2 or 3 stacked words, appearing as they're spoken: big, small, big. Big words are Unbounded Bold, lowercase, white on a blue block, the last one yellow. The small word is Poppins Bold, under half the size, white, no block. Two word stacks back to back make a contrast ("better videos / same tools").
- **Big number:** a money figure or count in Unbounded Bold on a blue block, while it's said.
- **Plain-words line:** anything technical on screen gets a line in plain words under it ("Remotion" / "makes the graphics"), in the same colour at about 60% opacity.
- **Term line:** a term or number the audience won't know ("AI agent", "trial reels"): the term with its plain-words line under it, dark on a white pill with a thick primary border, centred low in the frame whatever the layout. It slides up on the word and back down after its line.
- **Section card:** a new section or step. A dark screen with one centred title, and the plain-words line under it when the title is a tool or a technical term. Nothing above the title. It cuts on with the line that opens the section and swipes off downwards while the video and the voice carry on.

### Overlays

Whenever a company, app, place, product or scene is named, show it: a photo, or the brand's logo or app icon. Screen shares, clips and explainers get a layout instead (below).

- Hard edges, no border, no shadow.
- Several in a row for a list, one per item, changing on the spoken word.
- **App icons:** real icons, never name labels. Each slides in on its word from its own side, and they slide out together, mirrored around the centre. Four take the four corners in the order they're named. One or two sit one on each side, about twice the size.
- Place them where they read best, even if that covers the face.

### Layouts

Pick by what the speaker is doing. Switch layouts on a cut. Never hide the speaker to make room: the screen without them only when the user asks.

- **Full screen:** they explain an idea or give an opinion and there's nothing to show. Also the intro, the outro, asides and jokes.
- **Screen share:** the screen with the speaker beside it, whenever a screen recording has something to show. The shape sections say how the two sit.
- **Zoom into the screen** (not a punch-in on the speaker): when they point at or name something on it, like a button, a price or a command. Up to 3x. It goes in and out in about 0.5 s, soft at both ends. Hold it while they talk about it, then glide straight to the next thing without zooming out in between. Centre the target in the part of the frame the speaker's picture leaves free.
- **Split:** the frame in two, the speaker in one part and the thing shown in the other: screenshots, clips, explainers and lists they count off. Stacked in vertical, side by side in horizontal.
- **Behind:** when they talk about something with parts (a product reaching accounts, a hub and spokes) or show results: a motion graphic, or up to 2 pictures or muted clips, behind the speaker, who is cut out in front.

### Splits

For anything the speaker explains that isn't a single overlay: a screen share or screenshot, a video clip, a motion graphic or explainer. Also when they point or say "this" about something to show.

- As few layers as possible. Screenshots, screen recordings and clips fill their part edge to edge, cropped to fill. No frame, card or background around something that already has edges. Keep what they're talking about in view.
- Only things without edges of their own (icons, text, motion graphics) get a background: cream or dark, whichever stands apart from them, filling the part.
- The speaker's part shows them at their normal size, never zoomed in. Frame them for each split from the footage.
- Starts and ends on a cut. Holds as long as they talk about what's shown: the shown part switches to the next thing on the spoken word, and the split ends with that topic. Things named during a split go in the shown part, not over the face.
- A clip from someone else's video freezes on its moment before it cuts to anything off-topic (a presenter, a transition).
- **List:** for a list they count off ("two problems", "three steps"): a panel on the dark background with a title at the top left, then each point as a headline with its plain-words line, sliding in a short way on its line. Numbers sit in small primary circles.
- **Explainer:** for what a tool does, on cream: what goes in on the left, the tool's real icon in the middle, what comes out on the right, made from the real thing (their own voice as the waveform, their own words coming out). A title at the top left with its plain-words line ("Speech to text" / "sound in, words out").

### Pacing and cuts

- Tight jump cuts, no pauses between lines. J-cuts: the next line is heard before its picture shows.
- A jump cut is never bare: cover it with what's named, a card or the screen. If nothing fits, punch in to about 115% on every other take, eye line held. A section card resets the punch-in.
- Punch in only to hide a jump cut or to land a key line, never just to keep things moving. One framing per take otherwise. Splits never punch in on the speaker; zooming into the screen in the shown part is fine.
- Waiting (on an AI, a page, an upload) is cut out, never sped up.

### Motion

- Captions and splits cut in and out. Section cards cut on.
- Everything else gets a small, quick entrance, about 0.35 s, eased: a pop for something that appears on its own (an overlay, a word, a point), a slide for something that moves into place (a panel, an icon from its side, a card leaving). Never slow or floaty.
- Graphics act out the idea: lists build item by item on the spoken word, what gets cut leaves, before turns into after.
- Headlines land with a snap and hold still while they're read. The words of a word stack arrive one by one.
- No tilt, rotation or bounce, apart from the pictures behind the speaker.

### Sound

- No music, unless asked.
- Sound effects, subtle: their peaks 10 dB under the voice's.
  - A pop when an overlay, a word of a word stack, a point, a result or a term line lands.
  - A swoosh when something slides in or out, peaking in the middle of its move.
  - A click when the shown thing changes (the next overlay in a run, the shown part of a split switching) and on real clicks.
  - A thud when a section card cuts on.
  - A key press on real key presses, typing under code being typed.
  - A ding or a deny when an explainer lets something through or stops it.
- No sound on zooms, on cuts between the speaker and the screen, or per word.

## Vertical and square videos (9:16, 4:5, 1:1, etc)

Sizes are for a 1080x1920 frame; scale them with the width.

### Captions (the whole video)

- Poppins Bold, white, about 46px, one line, at most about 22 characters.
- On a small dark pill hugging the text: dark at 15% opacity, padding about 40% of the text size each side, corners about 25%.
- Short phrases of two to five words, the whole phrase at once, in time with the voice.
- All lowercase, even at the start of a sentence; only names, brands and "I" keep a capital. No full stops or commas; question and exclamation marks stay. Apostrophes stay: "don't", "it's".
- Centred, **bottom**. Stays put the whole video, also under overlays. During a split, centred on the seam.

### Text hook (first 3 to 5 seconds)

- Two lines, centred, **top**: a short lowercase lead-in in Poppins Bold (about 52px, white) saying what it is ("new Claude feature"), then the name in Unbounded Bold caps (about 76px, white on a blue block). No punctuation.
- Starts about 80px under the safe zone's top edge, never right on it. It may overlap the top of the head, never the eyes.
- Logos are welcome around the hook when the video is about a brand, kept clear of the text and the head. Nothing forced.
- Stays while the first sentences play, then cuts away.

### Sizes

- Word stack: big words about 96px, small words about 44px. Centred, **top**.
- Big number: about 120px. Centred, **top**.

### Overlays

- About 65% of the frame width, inside the safe zone, shifted left, centre or right from one overlay to the next. The caption stays visible below.

### Layouts

- **Full screen** (most of the video): the speaker from the chest up.
- **Screen share:** a split.
- **Split:** 50/50, stacked. Top half: the thing shown, filling it edge to edge, even past the safe zone. Bottom half: the speaker, moved up or down so the top of their head (hair, cap or hat included) sits about 5% of the half's height (about 50px) under the seam. Lists and explainers go in the top half.
- **Behind:** the motion graphic in the top half behind the speaker, no split.
- No insets or cutaways.

## Horizontal videos (16:9, etc)

Sizes are for a 1920x1080 frame; scale them with the width.

No hook and no captions unless explicitly asked for.

### Sizes


| Use                       | Font                  | Size                                                     |
| ------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Section card title        | Unbounded Bold        | 92px                                                     |
| Title of a panel or scene | Unbounded Bold        | 82px                                                     |
| A point in a list, a term | Unbounded Bold        | 50px                                                     |
| Plain-words line          | Poppins Medium        | 44px under a section card title, 34px under other titles |
| Number in a list          | Poppins Bold          | 26px                                                     |
| Code                      | JetBrains Mono Medium | 30px                                                     |


### Layouts

- **Screen share:** picture-in-picture. The screen recording fills the frame: no background, padding, rounded corners or shadow. The speaker is a small portrait (9:16) window, about 16% of the frame width, head and shoulders, corners about 8%, a soft shadow, never zoomed in. Two places, about 5% of the frame from the edge: **right, centred** top to bottom (the default), or **left, centred**. They move left only when they'd cover what's shown, and move on a cut.
- **Split:** side by side, the thing shown on the left, the speaker on the right half.
- **List:** the panel takes the left half, sliding in from the left as one piece and pushing the speaker to the right half, then sliding back out the same way. The push takes about 0.5 s.
- **Explainer:** the full frame on cream, the speaker's picture small on the right. It cuts on. When the next clip is the speaker full screen, their picture grows to full screen over it; otherwise it cuts.
- **Behind:** the pictures or clips slide in from either side, slightly tilted, with a white border and a soft shadow, and slide out together.
