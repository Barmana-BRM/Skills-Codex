# Crystalize Material Specification

## Material Identity

A warm, translucent, optically coherent crystalline medium combining
transparency, color, refraction, varied natural facets, internal illumination,
and organic structure. It should feel as though the pictured world naturally
formed from crystal, not as though crystal texture was applied afterward.

It should feel like naturally formed translucent crystal artwork, not
commercial glass or jewelry CGI. Glass/crystal identity must be immediately
visible; a result that reads only as watercolor, gouache, pastel, or collage
fails even when it contains polygonal shapes.

## Transparency

Spatially variable: - thin areas: high transparency - thick areas:
higher color density - backlit areas: greater translucency - shadowed
areas: deeper color

Never use uniform alpha.

Show enough clear overlap for the viewer to perceive what lies behind thin
facets. Combine transparent edge planes, denser cores, internal reflections,
and restrained double refraction. Do not reduce transparency merely to keep the
work illustrative.

## Color

Preserve source colors while translating them through the material: -
red → ruby / rose / translucent crimson - green → moss / sage /
emerald - blue → clear blue / glacier blue / muted sapphire - yellow →
amber / pale honey - brown → smoky amber / warm quartz - white → milky
crystal / clear quartz

## Facet Scale and Density

Use a three-level hierarchy: a few large structural planes for calm, varied
medium facets for form, and smaller facets at focal details, edges, refraction
zones, and material transitions. When the result feels fake, flat, or animated,
increase medium/small facet variation before adding more large triangles.
Never use equal-size tessellation, a dense universal mesh, or tiny glitter
noise.

## Facet Geometry

Prefer irregular polygons, softly angled planes, overlapping crystalline
planes, organic boundaries, and varied scale.

Avoid perfect triangles, uniform grids, hard diamond patterns, and
mechanical low-poly geometry.

## Internal Light

Subtle illumination may travel through the material, accumulate near
thin edges, illuminate interior planes, and brighten selected facets. Do
not make the entire object glow.

## Refraction

Use restrained refraction to communicate thickness, depth, curvature,
and transparency. Avoid constant strong distortion.

Use selective doubled contours, background displacement through thick areas,
internal reflections, and caustic light paths to make the material genuinely
clear rather than merely glossy.

## Dispersion

Rare and subtle. Small blue, violet, cyan, or warm-yellow shifts may
occur at selected optical transitions. Never make rainbow dispersion
dominant.

## Highlights

Localized, soft, thin, directional, and integrated with facets. Avoid
white outlines, thick rim lights, plastic gloss, and repeated specular
dots.

## Shadows

Contact shadows: soft and grounding. Transmitted shadows: lighter,
diffuse, partially transparent, optionally subtly colored, and allowed
to scatter beyond the silhouette.

## Shallow Dimensionality

The illustration may use beveled edges, overlapping layers, subtle parallax,
and tiny precise facet shadows, as if thin crystal pieces sit slightly above
the paper. Keep this tactile and restrained. Never turn the artwork into a
product render, freestanding sculpture, mineral cluster, or 3D diorama.

## Edge Material

Terminate the outer silhouette with crisp, rigid, individually readable facet
planes. Vary their angle, scale, completeness, and projection so the contour
stays organic rather than mechanically regular. Selected edges may show higher
transparency, narrow highlights, subtle refraction, tiny planes, and delicate
fragments, but every boundary against the paper remains sharp.

Never use watercolor feathering, pigment bleed, foggy halos, airbrushed fades,
soft alpha, torn paper fibers, or dry-brush edges. Never simulate crispness with
a uniform outline or thick glowing border.

## Material Hierarchy

Primary subject: full treatment. Secondary forms: reduced treatment.
Background: minimal or no crystalline treatment.

## Avoid

Broken/shattered glass, metallic crystal, plastic, hard ice-like
surfaces, jewelry-ad aesthetics, neon crystal, excessive glitter,
excessive bloom, uniform transparency, uniform facets, watercolor-only
appearance, flat animation, opaque vector blocks, mechanical low-poly geometry,
blurred or feathered boundaries.
