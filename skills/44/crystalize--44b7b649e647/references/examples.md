# Crystalize Examples

These are qualitative regression cases.

## Simple Object

Source: bottle and tissue box. Keep their identity, color, and essential relationship; remove wall texture and photographic noise. Give the primary object the richer crystalline treatment.

## Landscape Regression: Pale Mineral Terraces, Cyan Water, and Sky

**Incorrect:** Preserve the complete landscape composition and turn every
region into crystal. Also incorrect: isolate the terraces as a floating
milky-crystal sculpture on a dark field.

**Correct:** Extract the pale terraces, cyan pool, dark water edge, and soft sky
as anchors. Simplify them into broad, recognizable 2D layers inside one
irregular-edged horizontal vignette on warm cream paper. Paint those layers
with restrained translucent crystalline facets. Add a small title such as
`Water Over White` beneath the vignette.

## Reference-Pair Regression

When examples show a photograph paired with a simplified illustration, learn
the **method**: selective landscape extraction, broad flat shapes, partial
edges, paper field, and concise caption. Do not copy the reference's specific
mountains, roads, buildings, colors, or title. Apply that method to the user's
source and use crystal as the illustration medium.

## Orientation Regression

**Incorrect:** Convert a landscape source into a portrait poster, or a portrait
source into a wide template, without being asked.

**Correct:** Keep landscape sources landscape and portrait sources portrait.
Create originality inside that orientation through scale, crop, spacing,
overlap, simplification, and negative space.

## Natural Crystal Regression

**Incorrect:** Keep the original composition and add a uniform triangle filter.
Also incorrect: use a few oversized opaque polygons so the work reads as flat
animation, or increase realism until it becomes a crystal product render.

**Correct:** Redraw 2–5 anchors in a new editorial/journal arrangement. Use a
few large structural planes, varied medium facets, and smaller optical facets;
show transparency, refraction, caustics, doubled contours, and restrained
shallow-relief shadows. The pictured world should feel naturally crystalline
while remaining a paper-based illustration.

## Portrait Still-Life Regression

Source: two layered matcha drinks beside a sunny window.

**Incorrect:** Trace the room, glasses, plant, and table, then apply low-poly
facets. Also incorrect: make a horizontal advertisement or photoreal beverage
render.

**Correct:** Preserve portrait orientation. Extract the two drinks, straws,
plant, and warm light; recompose them as unequal crystal columns, simplified
leaf facets, clear window planes, and an amber light field on cream paper. Keep
the liquid layers recognizable and naturally translucent.

## Complex Editorial Reconstruction

For a visually dense photo, identify 2–5 anchors, discard clutter, simplify the
landscape into a few readable layers, and crystallize those layers without
turning them into a volumetric object. The result is an independently designed
paper-based art print, never “the original photograph, but made of crystal.”

## Landmark and Caption Regression

Source: a recognizable waterfront observation tower under dramatic clouds.

**Incorrect:** Render the scene as soft watercolor with a generic title such as
`Tower Under Clouds`, or assume the structure is a lighthouse without
verification. Use large upright type that competes with the image.

**Correct:** Verify the structure as the Erie Basin Marina Observation Tower
and title it `Erie Basin Marina`. Make glass clarity, transparency, internal
refraction, and hard irregular facet edges immediately visible. Set the title
in small, distinctive classical handwritten script, subordinate to the image.
